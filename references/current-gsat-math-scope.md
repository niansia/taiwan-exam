# Current GSAT mathematics curriculum gate

Use this gate for every Math A and Math B item. The controlling source is the CEEC mathematics examination specification for the 111 academic year onward, especially Appendix 1. CEEC defines Math A as grade-10 required mathematics plus grade-11 required Math A, and Math B as grade-10 required mathematics plus grade-11 required Math B. It also says a term or concept not common to all textbook versions must be explained in the paper.

## Required two-pass audit

1. **Content-code pass** — every item must record one or more `scope_codes` from the applicable list below. A context name is not scope evidence. Each scored operation in the official solution must be covered by at least one recorded code.
2. **Boundary pass** — inspect the prompt, options, diagram, expected solution, and distractor reasoning for a hidden theorem, term, or procedure. A familiar-looking vector, matrix, probability, or function does not pass merely because its chapter title is in scope.

Classify each item as one of these:

- `direct`: all assessed knowledge is explicitly in the applicable learning-content items.
- `defined-bridge`: an unfamiliar outside-world object or label is fully defined inside the item, and solving it requires only direct in-scope operations. The outside object itself must not become the assessed knowledge.
- `reject`: the item assumes or rewards knowledge outside the applicable list, uses an explicit exclusion, or cannot be solved without an unprovided method.

## Common grade-10 codes for Math A and Math B

- `N-10-1` real numbers and scientific notation
- `N-10-2` absolute-value equations and inequalities
- `N-10-3` exponents, geometric mean, and AM-GM
- `N-10-4` common logarithms; other logarithm bases are not a grade-10 direct target
- `N-10-5` numerical error; marked integration-only rather than a direct target
- `N-10-6` finite recurrences, finite geometric series, common sums, induction
- `N-10-7` propositions and necessary/sufficient conditions; integration-only rather than a direct target
- `G-10-1` listed coordinate symmetries only
- `G-10-2` lines, slopes, distances, parallel/perpendicular lines, linear inequalities in two variables
- `G-10-3` standard circle equation
- `G-10-4` line-circle relations and tangency; not relations between two circles
- `G-10-5` directed angles and polar coordinates
- `G-10-6` sine, cosine, and tangent from acute through directed angles
- `G-10-7` sine law, cosine law, projection, and basic triangulation
- `A-10-1` cubic identities, radical and rational-expression operations
- `A-10-2` factor and remainder theorems and division by `x-a`
- `F-10-1` linear and quadratic functions and contextual optimization on a closed interval
- `F-10-2` global/local graphical features and symmetry of cubic functions
- `F-10-3` linear, quadratic, or already-factored polynomial inequalities
- `D-10-1` elementary sets and Venn diagrams; integration-only rather than a direct target
- `D-10-2` one-variable summaries, scatterplots, best-fit line, correlation, standardization
- `D-10-3` systematic counting, trees, addition/multiplication/inclusion-exclusion, permutations and combinations
- `D-10-4` classical probability for compound events and expected value

## Explicit exclusions and subject boundaries

- Math A `A-11A-2` explicitly says not to extend Gaussian elimination to matrix rank and not to extend the discussion to linear independence. Therefore `線性獨立`, `線性相依`, basis/dimension, rank, and equivalent tested knowledge are release-blocking even if the numerical work resembles a three-variable system.
- Calculus limits, derivatives, and integrals are not in GSAT Math A or Math B.
- Eigenvalues/eigenvectors and general diagonalization are not in either GSAT mathematics subject.
- Math B does not inherit Math A. In particular, spatial vectors, three-dimensional plane equations, trigonometric addition formulae, plane linear transformations, and Math-A-style general logarithm-law manipulation cannot be assumed in Math B.
- Math B conics are visual plane-section concepts, not an unrestricted license for analytic conic equations.
- Any item using an unfamiliar scientific, technical, financial, or artistic mechanism must supply all outside facts needed. It may assess translation into an in-scope model; it may not assess the external field.

## Paper-level curriculum and context balance

The scope check is not complete until the whole paper is inspected. For a 20-question internal current-form paper:

- include pure mathematical items, neutral/daily-life applications, and dated/source-bearing literacy items;
- dated/source-bearing items should normally occupy about 25–40% of scored items, not dominate the paper;
- no single topical family such as spaceflight, AI, astronomy, energy, or sensors should exceed 20% of scored items;
- at least one counting/combinatorics item should appear unless the sampled multi-year blueprint gives a documented reason to omit it;
- a complete 20-item Math A paper must contain one or two items carrying `F-10-2`; two such items must use distinct mechanism families, and a routine coefficient substitution alone does not satisfy the depth audit;
- a contextual mixed group must still map every subpart to scope codes and must pass a source-removal audit; physical plausibility is not a substitute for mathematical scope.

Treat these percentages as an internal ecology gate derived from the user's target for balanced papers, not as a claim that CEEC fixes a public annual quota.
