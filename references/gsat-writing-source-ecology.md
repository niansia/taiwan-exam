# Current GSAT writing source ecology

Use this file during a current-form GSAT writing generation pass. It contains only aggregate selection rules. Do not open year-by-year topic lists, old prompts, or named historical source summaries while drafting.

## Controlling hierarchy

1. Current official 111–115 papers define the form and the two rhetorical jobs.
2. Same-period publisher mocks broaden the admissible source families, but cannot overrule official form.
3. Older papers may suggest a general evidence or literary form only. They do not set current wording, length, scoring, or source proportions.

## Two different source jobs

The admissible source pool is publisher-neutral. United Daily News is neither banned nor preferred; the same is true of every other newspaper, publisher, magazine, platform, archive and institution. Never turn a familiar or easily searchable outlet into the default source of either task. The transformed excerpt must fit the selected official length envelope, but length alone cannot promote a candidate past provenance, rights, carrier, semantic-hinge, material-dependence and role-fit review.

- Task 1 is evidence-led. Search for two or more independently verifiable materials whose relationship creates a real decision, comparison, qualification, or tension. Suitable families include authored nonfiction, reportage, cultural or social analysis, popular science, public records, tables, rules, field observations, and clearly attributed viewpoints. Government material is allowed, but it is one family rather than the default for the whole paper.
- Task 2 is voice-led. Search Chinese-language literary writing first: contemporary Chinese essays, new poetry, picture-book prose, literary nonfiction, nature writing, travel writing, cultural reflection, diaries, letters, interviews with a strong first-person voice, or other authored prose that contains concrete perception and an emotional or reflective turn.
- A translated foreign literary work is an occasional exception, not the baseline. It may advance only when a traceable published Chinese translation is available. Do not present a raw English webpage title or an English bibliography as the normal student-facing source line.
- Do not make both tasks institutional explainers, policy summaries, technology news, or other sources with the same impersonal voice.

## Search tournament

For each task, independently search a wide candidate pool, then compare candidates before drafting. Across a full paper, record at least eight candidates spanning four publishers and four unrelated domains. Do not encode an allowed/preferred publisher list, and do not let one publisher occupy more than half the pool merely because its archive is convenient. Record language, authorial voice, genre, publication date, source reliability, sensory or evidentiary density, copyright-safe transformation plan, and why the material is necessary to the eventual prompt.

For task 2, reject a candidate when any of these is true:

- it is merely a translated English web article without an established Chinese edition;
- its only usable content is an abstract moral or a fact summary;
- the prose has no concrete scene, image, action, voice, or perceptual shift;
- the prompt could be written unchanged after removing the source;
- it is chosen mainly because it resembles a past topic.

At least one finalist must be a recent Chinese-authored literary or reflective work. Prefer a Chinese-facing final stimulus unless a translated work clearly wins on literary fitness and can be cited through its Chinese publication.

## Transformation and originality

- Every printed event, datum, attributed view, and concrete anecdote maps to a frozen source record. The LLM may select, paraphrase, translate, juxtapose, compress, and create a new question, but may not invent a supposedly real case.
- Source choice, rhetorical tension, material sequence, required evidence operation, emotional route, and title must be newly designed. Source grounding is not permission to imitate an old prompt.
- A task-2 passage should preserve the source author's perceptual movement without copying its signature wording. The new prompt must admit more than one defensible life experience or interpretation.
- Do not force a fashionable topic into every paper. Contemporary relevance is one possible source property, not a quota.

## Student-facing typography

- When two passages require labels, use unobtrusive `甲` and `乙`. Do not print `材料一：` or `材料二：` as headings.
- Put the full-width parenthetical attribution at the end of the passage's final paragraph, in the same paragraph flow: `……。（改寫自作者〈篇名〉，《刊物或書名》，日期）`
- Do not create a detached, centered, indented, or bibliography-like source block, and do not insert a blank paragraph before the attribution.
- Print Chinese author, title, publication, and date forms. Keep the URL, access date, license details, and frozen snapshot in the audit record rather than on the student paper unless the source itself makes a URL necessary.
- Natural wrapping is allowed, but the renderer must keep the attribution visually attached to the final paragraph and must not orphan it on another page.

## Release gates

- Run source-grounding validation and the originality audit.
- Verify the student PDF visually, not just the JSON or HTML.
- Reject a paper if task 2 has no authored Chinese literary source or traceable published Chinese translation.
- Reject a paper if the two tasks share one narrow source domain or voice.
- Reject a full-paper source pool with fewer than eight candidates, fewer than four publishers or four domains, an encoded publisher whitelist/preference, or unexplained majority concentration in one publisher.
- Reject a paper if any attribution is a separate paragraph or if `材料一：` / `材料二：` appears.
